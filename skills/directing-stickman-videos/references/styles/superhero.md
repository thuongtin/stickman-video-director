## What the style is

A 2D cel-shaded superhero cartoon in the spirit of US Saturday-morning and after-school action shows: a small team of original costumed heroes with clear powers, one or two villains, big readable action (flying, hero landings, catches, shoves) and short punchy lines. The appeal is silhouette and pose, so the style lives or dies on clean anatomy in extreme poses, which is exactly where video models break.

Use it for short action stories, team-up gags, hero explainers (a hero who explains a fact while catching something) and kids' content. It is a poor fit for calm or intimate stories (use Style 4) and for data-heavy explainers (use Style 1 or 2A).

## Sources

Studied from memory of the shows, not re-viewed frame by frame in this research; nothing below claims more than that.

| Work | What it supports | Where it runs out |
|---|---|---|
| *Batman: The Animated Series* (1992–95) | Bold flat shapes, strong silhouettes, very few lines inside a shape, shadow as one hard dark shape | Night-time noir palette and dark backgrounds; this pack is bright daylight |
| *Superman: The Animated Series* (1996–2000) and *Justice League* (2001–04) | Simplified heroic anatomy (broad chest, tapered waist, small head), one cel-shadow tone, readable flying and landing poses | Adult casts only; no child proportions |
| *Teen Titans* (2003–06) | A mixed-age team, bigger and more expressive kid faces, comedy reaction beats between action | Its anime-influenced faces and chibi gags, which this pack forbids |
| *The Powerpuff Girls* (1998–2005) | Kid heroes with very big heads, flat city skyline backgrounds, impact bursts | On-screen words and narrator cards, which this pack forbids |
| *Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse* (2018) | Halftone dot texture as a comic-print accent on flat skies | 3D rendering and on-screen sound words; only the dot pattern is taken |

Only the drawing method is taken: line, fill, shadow, proportion, how action is shown. No character, costume, colour combination, emblem, prop or layout from these works is used, and no prompt names them.

## Nearest neighbour

`modern-tv-cartoon` (gated). Two differences a viewer can see: (1) this pack shades every shape with one hard-edged shadow tone, while `modern-tv-cartoon` has flat fills with no shading; (2) this pack draws jointed heroic bodies with visible elbows and knees and small heads (about six heads tall), while `modern-tv-cartoon` draws bean bodies with big heads and bendy noodle limbs.

## Originality

Original characters only. No costume, colour combination, emblem shape, mask shape, cape shape, name or catchphrase that points to an existing hero. Emblems are simple non-letter shapes (a half sun, a hexagon, a zigzag, an almond). Prompts never name a publisher, studio, show or hero, not even in the negatives; the negative line is the generic `no resemblance to any existing comic-book or film superhero, no famous costumes, emblems or color schemes`.

## Shared prompt blocks (frozen, verbatim in every clip)

Style and environment:

```text
Style and environment: a bold 2D cel-shaded superhero cartoon in the look of a modern Saturday-morning action show, original characters only. Characters are lightly stylized heroic proportions: heads about one sixth of their height, broad chests, tapered waists, strong hands in solid-color gloves, every shape outlined with one even thick dark navy line. Flat color fills with exactly one hard-edged darker shadow tone on the side away from the light, no gradients, no airbrush, no texture on skin or suits. Faces: large expressive eyes with a white highlight, thick angular eyebrows, simple line noses, clear readable mouths. Backgrounds are flat-color city skylines with simple outlined buildings and rows of square windows, a faint pale halftone dot pattern on the sky, and a plain grey street. Action is shown with shapes only: white speed lines behind fast movers, a jagged white-and-yellow impact star with no letters, small dust puffs and ground cracks on landings. Animation is snappy: strong anticipation before every big move, a fast action, then a held pose for a beat. The kid sidekick has a bigger head, about one fifth of her height.
```

After the character anchors:

```text
Every character has exactly two arms and two legs, each arm ending in one gloved hand with a thumb and four fingers; a moving limb replaces its previous pose. Limbs keep their length and bend smoothly at elbows and knees, never backward. Every character wears a plain white cuff band on the right wrist only. Capes and cloaks stay behind the body and never cover the face. Characters stay at least one head-width apart; a flying character passes clearly above other characters' heads, never through their bodies.
```

Dialogue line:

```text
Dialogue: Audio voiceover only, strictly no speech bubbles, no dialogue boxes, no caption boxes, no sound-effect words. Speak exactly the quoted lines once, in order; do not add, omit, paraphrase, repeat, caption, subtitle or display any words. The characters never speak: no character lip-syncs, their mouths stay closed or show only their expression while the narrator speaks.
```

Negative constraints (style bans, IP, text, and the five posture negatives from `posture-gate.md`):

```text
Negative constraints: no resemblance to any existing comic-book or film superhero, no famous costumes, emblems or color schemes, no letters on chests, no logos, no watermark, no visible text, no onomatopoeia words such as POW or BAM, no comic panels or speech balloons, no 3D render, no CGI, no photorealism, no anime or manga style, no gradients or painterly texture, no extra arms, legs or fingers, no duplicate characters, no extra people, no knees or elbows bending backward, no limbs changing length, no head turned past the shoulders, no swapped left and right hands, no white cuff band on the left wrist, no prop switching hands without a visible pass, no cape covering a face, no character passing through another character, no blood
```

## Core rules this style overrides

- **Ending.** Action shorts end on a payoff or a cliffhanger (a villain reveal), not on a spoken discussion question, like Style 3's setup → escalation → payoff.
- **Location.** One street or rooftop may hold all clips of a fight, because action continuity matters more than a new setting every ten seconds; change place between fights, not inside one.
- **Held poses.** A hero pose (landing, catch, victory) is held for about half a second at the end of a beat. That hold is a deliberate beat, not an idle character.

## Voice and music (frozen)

The shared narrator is kept (owner's decision, 2026-10-20: no character dialogue). The accepted male block from `voice-continuity.md`, verbatim in every clip; characters never speak and their mouths stay closed or show only their expression:

```text
Voice: One off-screen adult male narrator throughout. General American accent, warm clean baritone, medium-low pitch, gentle chest resonance, no raspiness or breathiness. Calm conversational delivery, restrained pitch variation, steady speaking pace, and close, dry studio sound. Keep the same vocal timbre, register, accent, pace, and recording quality throughout. Emotional progression belongs to the visuals and music; do not change the narrator's voice or switch to character dialogue.
```

Score, written once in clip 1:

```text
Audio: punchy orchestral action music with brass hits and snare at a steady medium-fast tempo; <SFX for the clip>
```

Clips 2 to N start their Audio line with:

```text
Audio: the identical punchy orchestral action music with brass hits and snare from clip 1 continues, same tempo and instrumentation; <SFX for the clip>
```

Villain beats get their weight from the same orchestra (a low brass and snare hit), not from a new instrument.

## Text policy

`none`. No words, letters or numbers in generated video: no sound-effect words, no chest letters, no signs, no captions. Titles or subtitles are added in post-production.

## Motion cadence

`smooth`, with strong key poses: anticipation, a fast action, then a held pose for about half a second.

## Camera

Follow the camera grammar in `storyboard-template.md`; name the move in every beat.

- Transition moves (default): hold, slow push-in, pull back to reveal, slow lateral pan following a flyer or runner, hard cut to a new shot size (a low street-level angle suits landings).
- Accent moves: whip pan for a villain reveal, fast push-in on a hero's face. At most one accent per clip and in no more than one third of the clips; the hook may add one.
- Hold the camera during every demanding body action: catches, hero landings, stomps, overhead lifts. Never pair them with camera shake, orbit or tilt; a shake may follow the impact in the next beat.
- The sample package uses: clip 1 hold → pull back → slow push-in; clip 2 hold → slow pan → hard cut and hold; clip 3 hold → whip pan (the only accent) → hard cut and hold.

## Style bans and posture limits (from the gallery, gate and eye review)

Each rule came from a pose that the code-drawn rig got wrong first; a video model faces the same geometry. Put the matching words in the beat.

- **Hero landing**: the near knee and the back foot's toes on the ground, the fist pressed flat to the street under the shoulder, head up. Write all three contacts (`right knee and left foot on the ground, right fist pressed to the street`). A hovering knee or a fist in the air is the commonest failure.
- **Catching something heavy overhead (adults)**: show it from the front, both palms flat under the bottom corners, the object as wide as the shoulders. From the side, the arms swing past the head and hide the face.
- **Catching something thrown at chest height**: one hand under it, the other on its far side, knees dip on contact.
- **Flying**: body flat, the far fist forward, the near arm along the body. A near fist forward crosses the face in side view.
- **Throwing a heavy object**: a two-handed chest shove, not an overhead throw; the overhead wind-up crosses the face.
- **Sprint start**: a set position with both hands on the ground under the shoulders and the back toes touching.
- **Skid stop**: the front heel dug into the ground, body leaning back.
- **Ground slam**: crouch and draw both fists back beside the hips, then drive them down into the street in front of the feet, spread over several beats. Raising the fists overhead in profile sweeps the near fist across the face, and a one-frame snap pops the shoulders.
- **A kid needs a child's face, not a shrunken adult's**: a bigger round head (about one fifth of her height), large round eyes set low, a tiny dot nose, a small mouth close under the eyes, pink cheeks, bangs. The anchor carries those words; "eight-year-old" alone is not enough.
- **The kid cannot lift overhead**: her head is taller than her reach. She holds small things in front of her belly with both hands on the bottom corners, tossed in from the side so they never pass in front of her face.
- **Masks are domino masks**: one band across the eyes with the whites always visible. Round lenses joined by a bridge read as glasses; a mask filled solid over the eyes reads as sunglasses.
- **Victory / cheer**: arms out in a wide V, not straight up; straight arms cover a kid's big hood and face.
- **Standing hero pose (front)**: fists on the outer hips with elbows out, never on the belly; leave a gap between arm and waist.
- **Flying over others**: give the flight path a height (`passing well above Gọng Sắt's head`) and land in an empty spot.
- **Capes and collars** stay behind the head in side view.

## Hands

Follow `posture-gate.md`. Name every held prop with the character's own hand, one character per hand sentence, one noun per prop everywhere (`steel barrel`), and a sentence with a verb for every set-down or pass (`sets the steel barrel down on the street, then lets go of it`). Powers come from a named hand (`a swirl of ink smoke curls up from his left palm`).

## Hooks that fit

Reaction before cause (a hero braced and staring, then the thing flies in), Sudden entry (a speedster streaks in, a flyer swoops in from the top), Mid-action open (the first frame is already a catch or a landing). The hit is one impact star and one clang, not a screen-filling flash. The first narrator line is four to ten words with no greeting ("Some heroes see trouble coming a block away."). Do not open on a team lineup with nothing happening.

## Post-production

1. Join the clips with hard cuts at the seams; check that the score continues across each seam and trim a few frames if a beat repeats.
2. Mix the narration on top and duck the music under each line; normalize loudness across clips.
3. Add any title, credits or subtitles in post, in the bottom safe area, never in the generation prompt.
4. Review each clip at the risky moments (the catch, the landing, two characters in one shot): limb count, the white cuff band on the right wrist, faces not covered, no words or emblems that look like letters.
5. Check Đá Núi in the first clip for drift toward a known orange stone-skinned strongman; if it drifts, strengthen "burnt-orange bodysuit", "deep brown skin" and "short black beard" in his anchor rather than naming anyone.

## Risks on Omni Flash (untested)

1. Franchise drift (a caped flyer, the orange strongman); check the first frame of each character.
2. Generated sound-effect words on impacts and letters on chests.
3. Extreme poses (landing, overhead catch, flying) are where limb counts and left/right break; keep one demanding action per character per beat.
4. Capes clipping through bodies or covering faces when a character turns.
5. Two fast movers in one shot may merge; stage the second above or far from the first.
6. Characters moving their mouths as if they speak the narrator's lines.
