A bright, friendly 2D micro-world inside a blood vessel, seen as a cheerful look through a microscope. The cast are
original cell creatures, not people. Use it for how-the-body-works explainers: immunity, blood, wound healing,
infection, vaccines, sleep and repair, one idea per clip, classroom-safe. It fits poorly with medical advice, real
anatomy, surgery or anything that needs human characters; use science-lab or Style 4 there.

## Sources (never paste into prompts)

Studied from general knowledge of these works, not frame by frame. Treat every claim as a recollection, not a
measurement. Cell facts were checked against histology teaching pages (README.md, "Nguồn").

| Work | What it supports | Where it runs out |
|---|---|---|
| *Once Upon a Time... Life* (French animated series, 1987) | Body cells and antibodies as recurring characters with jobs, one body process per episode, a friendly classroom tone | Its cells are little people in uniforms and vehicles; ours are cell-shaped creatures with no clothes |
| Kurzgesagt immune-system explainers (YouTube, 2014 onward) | Cells as simple rounded shapes with tiny faces, a strong colour code per cell type, a calm VO over continuous animation | Flat vector fills with no translucency; its bird mascots and exact palette are trade dress to avoid |
| Amoeba Sisters biology videos (YouTube, US classrooms) | Friendly hand-drawn cells and microbes with faces, a nucleus visible inside, short clear explanations for students | Simple flat colouring and drawn presenters; no soft depth of field or vessel set |
| *Osmosis Jones* (2001) and *Cells at Work!* (2015 manga, 2018 anime) | Proof that a body-as-world story with heroes and germs holds a long story | Both draw cells as humans (cop, uniformed workers). Named only as what this style must **not** look like |

## Core rules this style overrides

- **Fill and light.** The shared defaults lean to flat fills (Style 1 Light forbids gradients). Here a soft gradient,
  translucency and a glossy highlight are the `must` traits: without them the cells read as flat stickers, and the
  visible nucleus is what tells a viewer which cell is which.
- **Faces.** The cells have small cartoon faces with big glossy eyes, unlike Style 1's blank or line faces: the cast
  has no costume, hair or hands-free gesture vocabulary, so the face and the whole membrane carry emotion.
- **Cast without human form.** No human characters at all, and germs have no limbs. The posture gate covers only the
  six humanoid cells; the germs are kept on model by the shape rule check instead of a rig.
- **Swallowing.** A macrophage engulfing a germ is a hug, then a hard cut to the germ sealed in a clear bubble inside
  the macrophage. A continuous swallow is a morph, which the contract bans.
- **Sitting.** The legs are too short for a seat: characters sit on the floor, membrane on the ground.
- **Locations.** One vessel set; a new location (lung, skin surface) is a planned hard cut named in Phase A.

## Text policy

`post-overlay`. Nothing is written in frame (no labels, letters, numbers or formulas). Cell names and terms such as
"B cell" or "antibody" are added in post-production.

## Motion cadence

`smooth`. Membranes squash, stretch and ripple continuously; stepped or on-twos motion makes the jelly look stiff.

## Camera moves

Follows the camera grammar in `storyboard-template.md`. Name one move per beat.

- Transition moves (default): hold, slow push-in, pull back, slow lateral track or pan along the vessel floor, hard
  cut to a new shot size.
- Accent moves: one fast push-in, only on the hook or a reveal; at most one accent per clip and in no more than a third
  of the clips. No orbit (the side-on floor stage breaks) and no camera shake on a cell that is throwing, catching or
  passing a prop.
- Hold during demanding body actions: two-hand holds and handoffs, the throw, the catch, the net stretch.
- Do not make the main move of three clips in a row an inward move; pull back or track sideways in between.
- The sample package: clip 1 fast push-in on the hole (hook), hold, slow pull back; clip 2 hold, slow lateral track,
  hold; clip 3 hold, hard cut to a medium shot, hard cut back and hold.

## Style bans and posture limits

- Side-on along the vessel floor, full-body framing; front view for reactions, cheers and two-handed holds.
- Short arms: a raised hand only reaches about eye level on the small cells, and a squat cell's raised hand reaches
  its cheek, so hands that catch or receive stay forward at chest height. Never put a hand over the eyes.
- Throws swing back at hip height, never cocked behind the head (the near arm would cross the face).
- Big two-handed props (oxygen bubble, net, a caught germ) are held against the belly, close enough to reach.
- Emotion goes through the whole membrane, not only the face.
- The real immune response takes hours to days; say so in Phase A and keep the VO honest.

## Voice and music

- Voice: the shared tested baritone block from `voice-continuity.md`, verbatim in every clip. The style never
  recasts the narrator and the cells never speak.
- Score, frozen. Clip 1: `a light plucked-string melody with soft bubbly synth pops`, then that clip's sound effects. Clips 2 to N open their Audio line with
  `the identical light plucked-string melody with soft bubbly synth pops continues from clip 1, same tempo and instrumentation;` followed by that clip's sound effects. The music
  never speeds up or resolves on its own; a final chord is written as a sound effect at the end of the last clip.

## Hook overrides

Sudden entry (a splinter or germ bursts into a calm vessel), Reaction before cause (a cell freezes and stares
off-frame), Scale shock (a cell next to a giant splinter tip or dust grain), Partial reveal (start on a mitten hand
holding the oxygen bubble, pull back). Keep the hit sound soft and bubbly or woody, never a bass drop.

## Post-production

1. Stitch the clips in order; trim at the planned hard cuts only.
2. Add term labels ("red blood cell", "antibody") as post overlays in the upper third, never over a face or a hand.
3. Add subtitles from the exact VO script.
4. Conform the plasma background colour across clips, and check that the score has no jump at each seam.
5. Normalize loudness; keep sound effects under the voice.
