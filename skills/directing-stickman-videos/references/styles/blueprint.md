An animated engineering blueprint: white construction lines on Prussian-blue cyanotype paper. Use it for science,
engineering, invention and "how it works" explainers, one mechanism per clip. It fits poorly with emotional
storytelling, nature, food or anything that needs colour coding; use science-lab or Style 4 there.

## Sources (never paste into prompts)

Studied from general knowledge of these works, not frame by frame. Treat every claim as a recollection, not a
measurement.

| Work | What it supports | Where it runs out |
|---|---|---|
| Cyanotype process (John Herschel, 1842) and the engineering and architectural blueprints made with it until the mid-20th century | The one-pigment look: Prussian-blue ground, white lines, uneven exposure, fold creases, the title block and border | Real prints are dense with lettering and numbers; we keep only the blank cells and capsules |
| Anna Atkins, *Photographs of British Algae: Cyanotype Impressions* (1843) | The depth and mottling of the blue, white shapes with soft edges where the paper was exposed | Photograms of plants, not line drawings; no construction marks or figures |
| ANSI/ASME Y14.2 "alphabet of lines" (line conventions for technical drawings) | Dash-dot centre lines, dashed hidden lines, 45-degree section hatching, dimension lines with arrowheads, two line weights | A standard for static drawings; says nothing about figures, faces or motion |
| US patent drawings of machines (late 19th to 20th century) | Cutaway housings in hatched section, gear trains, one part drawn alone to explain it | Black ink on white paper; their reference numerals are exactly the text this style must leave out |

## Signature traits

| # | Tag | Trait (visible in a still) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | must | Cyanotype ground: deep Prussian blue with uneven exposure, faint fold creases, a fine drafting grid with a heavier fifth line, a thin double border and an empty title block |
| 2 | must | One pigment: only Prussian blue and the white of the paper; fills are flat washes a shade lighter or darker than the ground |
| 3 | must | Draftsman's marks on every drawing: pin-joint circles with cross-hairs at shoulders, elbows and knees, dash-dot centre lines, 45-degree hatching, dimension lines whose labels are blank capsules, head guides |
| 4 | bonus | Machines in cutaway with gear trains inside, and new parts plotted on line by line from left to right |
| 5 | forbidden | Any other colour, gradients, 3D or glossy shading, and any letters, numbers, measurements or labels in frame |

## Nearest neighbour

whiteboard-doodle (research pack, `gated`). Visible differences:
1. Inverted ground: white lines on Prussian-blue paper with a grid, border and title block, against dark marker
   lines on a plain white board.
2. Construction marks on the figures themselves (pin joints with cross-hairs, centre lines, hatching), and drawing
   appears by a mechanical plotter sweep, against loose doodles drawn on by a visible hand and marker.

science-lab is the nearest by topic (science explainers) but is a full-colour cel-shaded lab with human scientists;
blueprint has no colour, no lighting and no room set.

## Frozen style block

Pasted verbatim into every clip, right after the output spec (and after the opening hook in clip 1):

```text
Style and environment: an animated engineering blueprint in flat 2D. The whole frame is cyanotype paper: deep Prussian-blue ground with slightly uneven exposure, faint fold creases, and a fine pale-blue drafting grid with a heavier line every fifth square, a thin double white border and an empty ruled title block in the lower right corner. Everything is drawn in crisp pale white lines of one or two weights over flat, slightly lighter blue fills; there is no other color. Draftsman's marks stay on every drawing: small pin-joint circles with cross-hairs at shoulders, elbows and knees, faint dash-dot centre lines through limbs and machines, dashed hidden lines for parts behind a wall, 45-degree section hatching on cut surfaces and work clothes, dimension lines with arrowheads whose labels are blank rounded capsules, and faint head guides (a centre line and an eye line) running just past each head. Machines are shown in cutaway: housings in hatched section with gear trains inside. New machine parts appear by being plotted on line by line, left to right, never by fading or morphing. Characters stand in flat side or front view on two white ground lines; the back row stands higher, not smaller.
```

## Core rules this style overrides

- **Palette.** The shared defaults allow up to three saturated accent colours. Here there are none: the single
  Prussian blue and white is the style, and any accent colour breaks the cyanotype.
- **Construction marks on bodies.** Core figures are clean lines. Here pin-joint circles, centre lines and head
  guides stay on every figure: they are the signature, and they make each bend visible at its joint, which helps
  the model keep elbows and knees bending the right way.
- **Text zones.** The default is no visible writing at all. Here the frame keeps blank dimension capsules and an
  empty title block on purpose, so real numbers and a title can be laid in during the edit (`blank-zone`).
- **Appearing parts.** The contract bans morphs; this style goes further: a new part is plotted on line by line from
  left to right, never faded in, so "appears" is never written in a beat.
- **Big heads.** Nova and Cog have large heads, so their pointing and waving gestures stay about 20 degrees lower
  than an adult's and an arm never crosses the face. Hank's overhead swing stops with the hands at chin height in
  front of the face and only the hammer handle rising.

## Text policy

`blank-zone`. Nothing is written in frame. The dimension-line capsules and the title block in the lower right are
left empty as reserved zones; numbers, part names and the title go into them in post-production.

## Motion cadence

`smooth`. Figures and gears move continuously; only the plotter sweep that draws a new part is mechanical.

## Camera moves

Follows the camera grammar in `storyboard-template.md`. Name one move per beat.

- Transition moves (default): hold, slow pull back, slow lateral track along the machine, hard cut to a new shot
  size. The sheet is flat, so tracks run parallel to it.
- Accent moves: one fast push-in on a reveal (a part seating, the gear train starting); at most one accent per clip
  and in no more than a third of the clips. No orbit, tilt or camera shake (the flat sheet and grid would break).
- Hold during demanding body actions: the gear handoff, lifting a part onto a shaft, two-hand carries.
- Do not make the main move of three clips in a row an inward move.
- The sample package: clip 1 hold tight then slow pull back (hook), hold, slow lateral track; clip 2 slow lateral
  track, hold through the pass, hard cut to a medium shot; clip 3 hold, one fast push-in at the click, slow pull back.

## Style bans and posture limits

- Flat side or front views on two white ground lines; the back row stands higher on the page, not smaller.
- Hand work (wrenches, valves, levers, handoffs) in medium shot, in open space beside the machine, never behind a
  housing.
- Two-handed props: say "in both hands" and keep the other hand free for the whole beat. A pass is three sentences:
  giver holds out, receiver grips "with both hands", giver lets go. Each holder grips their own side of the rim.
- Lifts from the floor are squat lifts (knees and hips bend, back inclined, load between the knees).
- Valve wheels and other two-hand grips sit wider than the shoulders with the elbows out, so the forearms never cross
  over the chest.
- Overhead swings keep the hands below the eyes; the tool rises, not the arms.

Negatives, verbatim at the end of every clip; they include the five shared posture negatives from `posture-gate.md`
("no knees or elbows bending backward", "no limbs changing length", "no head turned past the shoulders", "no swapped left and right hands", "no prop switching hands without a visible pass") and "no logos":

```text
Negative constraints: no letters, words, numbers, measurements, formulas, labels, logos or signatures anywhere, including dimension labels, title block, clipboard and machine panels (they stay blank); no colors other than Prussian blue and white; no 3D render, no CGI, no glossy shading, no gradients, no realistic anatomy, no photographic textures, no anime eyes, no extra arms or legs, no duplicate characters, no extra workers or crowds, no knees or elbows bending backward, no limbs changing length, no head turned past the shoulders, no swapped left and right hands, no white cuff on the left wrist, no prop switching hands without a visible pass, no morphing, no speech bubbles, no dialogue boxes, no watermark.
```

## Voice and music

- Voice, verbatim in every clip:

```text
Voice: One off-screen adult female narrator throughout. Neutral American accent, warm clear mid-range voice, soft forward resonance, no raspiness or breathiness. Curious, friendly explainer delivery, restrained pitch variation, steady unhurried pace, and close, dry studio sound. Keep the same vocal timbre, register, accent, pace, and recording quality throughout. Emotional progression belongs to the visuals and music; do not change the narrator's voice or switch to character dialogue.
```

- Score, frozen: clip 1 `a light ticking clockwork rhythm and soft pizzicato strings`; clips 2 to N open their Audio
  line with "seamlessly continues the identical ticking clockwork rhythm and soft pizzicato strings from clip 1, same
  tempo and instrumentation;" and then that clip's sound effects (plotter scratches, metal clicks, a whir).

## Hook overrides

Partial reveal (tight on hands at a mechanism, pull back as the machine plots on), Satisfying motion (a gear train
starting, a line plotted perfectly) and Reaction before cause. The sound cue is a plotter scratch, a single metal
click or a whir, not a bass hit. No counters or numbers on screen; any statistic goes into a blank capsule in post.

## Post-production

1. Stitch the clips in order; trim only at the planned hard cuts.
2. Fill the blank dimension capsules with the real numbers or part names the VO mentions, in a thin white technical
   font sized to the capsule; leave capsules the VO does not mention empty.
3. Fill the empty title block with the video title and episode, in the same font.
4. Add subtitles from the exact VO script, below the lower ground line and clear of the title block.
5. Conform the Prussian blue and the grid position across clips so the sheet does not jump at seams; check the score
   has no jump either.
6. Normalize loudness; keep sound effects under the voice.
