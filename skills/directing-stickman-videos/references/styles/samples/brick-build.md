# Brick-build sample package: "The top row pops" (3 clips, 16:9, ~30 s)

Six original brick-built townsfolk of Phố Khối; this story uses Bác Vữa, Bé Nút, Cô Bột, Ông Tạ and Bà Rêu. Hands are named from each figure's own side; every figure wears its lime-green ring on the right wrist. A knob hand holds only a prop with a round bar (the baguette, the wick pole, the watering can handle); a brick has no bar, so it is clamped between both knob hands. The hook follows the Golden Hook section of references/storyboard-template.md (pattern: Physics break; payoff in Clip 3). The brand name of toy bricks is never written in the specs. Not yet built into a full rendered video.

## Global continuity block

- Ratio and length: 16:9, three clips of about ten seconds (about 30 s), 24 FPS, 1080p.
- Style: `brick-build` (real brick stop-motion on a studded baseplate, draft pack `brick-build.md`, status gated). The style paragraph, the figure rule, the joint rule and the ring line are repeated verbatim in every clip.
- Cast: Bác Vữa (clips 1 and 3), Bé Nút (clips 1–3), Cô Bột (clip 2), Ông Tạ (clip 2), Bà Rêu (clip 3). Each anchor is the frozen text from `src/rig.py`, repeated verbatim in every clip the figure appears in; a clip lists exactly who is in it.
- Locks: one lime-green ring on the right wrist of every figure; knob hands hold only round bars (baguette, wick pole, watering-can handle); a brick is clamped between both knob hands and is listed on the clip's "Two-hand props" line; joints click only where the joint rule says; Bà Rêu has no legs.
- Palette: glossy primary plastic (brick red, tan, lime green, dark grey, pale blue backdrop) on a green baseplate with a grey road; each figure keeps the colours of its anchor in every clip.
- Narrator: one off-screen adult male narrator, General American accent, warm clean baritone. The voice block is identical in all three clips; no character dialogue.
- Score: light marimba and soft woodblock theme at a gentle walking tempo, set in clip 1 and continued unchanged in clips 2–3; plastic ticks on pose changes are sound effects, not score.
- Continuity: the red brick goes Bác Vữa → Bé Nút → Bác Vữa in clip 1; clip 2 is a time cut to dusk at the bakery; clip 3 is a time cut back to the rebuilt wall, where the red brick is seated for the payoff of the hook.
- Text: none in the frames. Captions or a title are added in post-production (text policy `none`).

## Stitching guide

1. Clip 1 → Clip 2: approved time cut to dusk at the bakery. What continues: Bé Nút with empty hands, the town, the score. What changes: place and light, Bác Vữa and the red brick leave, Cô Bột and Ông Tạ join. Hard cut on Bé Nút's last held pose; a 4–6 frame audio crossfade on the marimba.
2. Clip 2 → Clip 3: approved time cut back to the wall. What continues: Bé Nút with empty hands (he has handed nothing on; the baguette stays in clip 2), the wall and the red brick, now held by Bác Vữa in both hands. What changes: daylight returns, Bà Rêu joins with the watering can. Hard cut; no crossfade on the picture.
3. Trim each clip to a held pose: stop-motion holds every pose for two frames, so cut on the second frame of a hold, never mid-click. Conform all clips to 24 FPS before joining.
4. Inspect each real join: if a rendered clip ends on a different grip, the ring on the wrong wrist, or a figure on the other side of the frame, trim to the last frame where the hands match the "Hands at end" line rather than trusting the spec.

## Voice and music continuity note

- Every clip repeats the same narrator paragraph word for word, and clips 2–3 say the marimba and woodblock theme continues from clip 1 with the same tempo and instrumentation. Check by listening to the three clips back to back, not from stills.
- Record the narration as one VO track with this voice block, cut it to the beats, and lay the marimba as one continuous bed over the assembled picture.
- Post-production: lay the plastic tick effects on their beats; add any caption or title as an overlay on the pale blue backdrop, never in the specs; a light grade is fine, but do not smooth or interpolate frames (it breaks the on-twos stop-motion).
